# Nokta Simplex Lite vs Garrett Ace 400: Budget Detector Showdown

*Last updated: June 2026 · By Wasatch Rockhound · 7 min read*

These are the two detectors every new rockhound asks about first, and for good reason — they're the most-recommended budget options in the hobby, sitting $100 apart in price but taking genuinely different approaches to that lower price tier. The Simplex Lite bets on waterproofing and dead-simple usability. The Ace 400 bets on Iron Audio and mineralized-ground performance.

If you searched for either of these by name, you're probably trying to decide between them right now. Here's the honest breakdown.

## Where the Nokta Simplex Lite Wins

**Waterproofing is the headline feature, and it's real.** Submersible to 16 feet, the Simplex Lite goes places the Ace 400 simply can't — creek beds, shallow washes, hunting right after rain without worrying about the control box. At this price point, nothing else on the market matches that depth rating.

**$100 cheaper for a genuinely capable detector.** That's not a small gap. For a brand-new rockhound who isn't sure detecting is going to stick, $249 is a meaningfully lower bar to clear than $349 — and you're not getting a toy for that money.

**Simpler out of the box.** The single-menu, turn-on-and-go design means you're swinging and finding targets within minutes of opening the box. There's less to learn before you're productive in the field, which matters if your goal is getting out there fast rather than studying a manual first.

**Rechargeable battery removes a real annoyance.** USB charging means no trips to buy AAs, no dead batteries mid-hunt because you forgot to restock. On multi-day trips especially, this is a genuine quality-of-life win.

**Lighter on the arm.** At 2.6 lbs versus 2.8 lbs, the gap is small on paper but adds up over a 5-6 hour hunting day. If arm fatigue has ever cut a trip short for you, it's worth factoring in.

---

## Where the Garrett Ace 400 Wins

**Iron Audio is a genuine differentiator the Simplex Lite doesn't have.** This is the single biggest functional gap between these two machines. Iron Audio lets you hear iron targets through a distinct tone separate from non-ferrous targets — meaning you learn to sort junk from potential finds by ear, without staring at the screen. In Utah's mining-debris-littered desert, this isn't a nice-to-have. It's the difference between digging fifty pieces of rusted junk and digging the right five.

**The DD coil handles mineralized desert soil better.** Utah's volcanic and alkaline soil is exactly the kind of ground that causes false signals and erratic readings on detectors not built for it. The Ace 400's coil and ground handling were specifically engineered around this kind of terrain, and it shows in the field.

**Build quality feels more substantial.** At nearly the same price tier, the Garrett simply feels like it's built to take a beating — tighter shaft connections, a more secure coil mount, more confidence-inspiring construction for rocky, rough terrain use.

**The support ecosystem is enormous.** Garrett's US-based customer service plus the sheer size of the Ace 400 user community means you're never stuck. More YouTube tutorials, more forum threads, more local detecting club members running this exact machine than almost anything else in the hobby.

## For Utah Rockhounding Specifically

This is where the generic spec comparison stops mattering and the terrain-specific answer takes over.

**If you're hunting dry desert and BLM open land — the majority of Utah rockhounding:** The Ace 400 is the stronger pick. Iron Audio earns its keep in mining-debris-heavy ground, and the coil's mineralized-soil performance is built for exactly this kind of terrain. You're not paying for waterproofing you won't use.

**If creek beds, washes, or hunting after rain are part of your plan:** The Simplex Lite wins outright — the Ace 400 cannot go in water at all, full stop. Sixteen feet of waterproofing covers far more than the occasional wet wash; it means you can hunt with zero anxiety about weather or terrain.

**If you're not sure detecting is going to stick and want the lowest-risk entry point:** Start with the Simplex Lite. At $249 with genuine capability and a forgiving learning curve, it's the easier first purchase to justify. You can always step up to a mineralized-ground specialist like the Ace 400 — or further, the [Garrett AT Max](/review/garrett-at-max) — once you know the hobby has hooked you.

**If you're committed and want the detector that will teach you the most about reading targets:** The Ace 400's Iron Audio feature is a genuine skill-building tool. Owners consistently report it sped up how quickly they learned to distinguish trash from targets by ear — a skill that pays off on every detector you ever own after this one.

---

## The Verdict

For most new rockhounds hunting Utah's dry desert and BLM land, **the Garrett Ace 400 is the better long-term investment.** Iron Audio teaches you the hobby faster, the mineralized-ground performance matches the terrain you're actually standing on, and the $100 premium buys real, terrain-specific capability rather than features you won't use.

The **Nokta Simplex Lite is the smarter buy if water is anywhere in your plans**, or if you want the lowest-risk way to find out whether detecting is for you before committing more money. Sixteen feet of waterproofing at $249 is a spec you won't find anywhere else near this price.

Both are excellent first detectors. Neither will hold you back while you're learning — they'll only start to feel limiting once you already know enough to want something more specialized, and by then you'll know exactly which direction to go.

## FAQ

**Which is better for a complete beginner?**
Both are beginner-friendly, but in different ways. The Simplex Lite has the shorter learning curve out of the box — fewer settings, less to configure before you're detecting. The Ace 400 takes a bit longer to feel fluent with, but Iron Audio teaches you to actually read targets faster once you do, which pays off over time.

**Can I use either of these for gold prospecting?**
Neither is purpose-built for gold the way the [Garrett Goldmaster 24K](/review/garrett-goldmaster-24k) is. Both can find larger gold pieces, but if gold prospecting is your primary goal rather than general rockhounding, a dedicated gold machine will outperform either of these.

**Is the $100 price difference worth it?**
It depends entirely on terrain. If you're exclusively hunting dry Utah desert and BLM land, the Ace 400's mineralized-ground performance and Iron Audio justify the premium. If water is part of your plan at all, the Simplex Lite's waterproofing makes the cheaper price an even better deal — you're getting more capability for less money in that scenario.

**Do I need Iron Audio?**
Not strictly, but it's a meaningful time-saver in trashy or mining-debris-heavy sites — which describes a lot of Utah's historic mining areas. If you're hunting clean, open BLM land away from old mining activity, you'll notice the gap less.
