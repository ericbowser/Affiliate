# U-Dig Fossils Utah: Complete Trilobite Quarry Guide

*Last updated: August 2026 · By Wasatch Rockhound · 8 min read*

U-Dig Fossils is the odd one out on this list — it's not free BLM land, it's a private 40-acre quarry in the Wheeler Shale, and you pay by the hour to dig. It earns its spot anyway because the payoff is close to guaranteed: this is one of the most prolific trilobite deposits in North America, and the staff hand you the tools and show you exactly how to split shale to find them.

If Topaz Mountain and Dugway are about self-reliance in remote desert, U-Dig is the opposite — guided, managed, and built for people who want a sure thing. That makes it the best pick on this list for kids, first-timers, or anyone traveling with someone who isn't a dedicated rockhound.

## Quick Facts

- **Location:** Near Antelope Springs, Millard County, Utah
- **GPS:** 39.3548°N, 113.2787°W
- **Distance from SLC:** ~2.5–3 hours (~182 miles)
- **Land Status:** Private quarry — fee required
- **Season:** March 30 – October 24 (2026)
- **Hours:** Mon–Sat, 9 AM–6 PM. Closed Sundays and July 4th
- **Difficulty:** Easy — tools provided, staff-guided
- **Vehicle:** Any vehicle — well-maintained gravel road

## What You'll Find

Trilobites — extinct marine arthropods that lived roughly 500 million years ago, when this part of Utah sat beneath a shallow inland sea. The Wheeler Shale here is one of the most productive trilobite deposits anywhere in the world.

**Elrathia kingii** — The most commonly found species, typically under an inch, beautifully preserved in the dark shale.

**Asaphiscus wheeleri** — Less common than *Elrathia*, larger, and a nice step up when you find one.

**Peronopsis interstricta** — A tiny agnostid trilobite, easy to miss but a fun find once you know what to look for.

Specimens range from about 1/8 inch to 2 inches. Everything you find is yours to keep — there's no daily weight limit like BLM sites, since this isn't public land.

## How to Get There

**From Salt Lake City:** Roughly 182 miles / 2.5–3 hours via US-6 through Delta.

**From Delta:** Head west on Highway 6/50 for 32 miles. Watch for the U-Dig Fossils sign between mile markers 56 and 57, then turn north. Follow the well-maintained gravel road for 20 miles — U-Dig signage marks the route the entire way — to reach the quarry.

**GPS coordinates:** 39.3548°N, 113.2787°W. This site has no street address; use these coordinates or the marked turn off Highway 6/50, not a mapping app's address search.

**Phone:** (435) 864-3638 — worth having saved in case you lose the signage trail.

There's no fuel or services past Delta, so fill up before you head west.

## What Happens When You Arrive

Check in at the quarry office — no reservation required, though booking online in advance is available if you'd rather lock in a time. Staff provide hammers, chisels, and a bucket, walk you through the splitting technique, and point you toward productive ground. Kids get particular attention here; this is a genuinely kid-friendly operation, not just a site that tolerates children.

The technique itself is simple and a little meditative: split the shale along its natural bedding planes with a chisel, and check each new surface for the telltale outline of a trilobite before moving to the next layer. Staff typically get you started with a few finds in your bucket before turning you loose.

**Gate closes promptly at 6 PM.** If you want to dig up to closing, plan to arrive by 3:45 PM at the latest.

## Pricing

Rates are tiered by how long you dig — 2-hour, half-day (4 hour), and full-day (8 hour) options, with separate youth pricing (ages 7–16) and free admission for kids 6 and under with a paying adult. A 15% discount applies for Utah residents, active military, veterans, and seniors 70+ (discounts don't stack).

Exact current rates weren't reliably pulled from their booking system as of this writing, and older third-party write-ups disagree with each other on the numbers — **confirm pricing directly at [u-digfossils.com/rates-booking](https://u-digfossils.com/rates-%26-booking) before you go.** As a rough planning number, expect somewhere in the $30–$90 per-adult range depending on how long you stay.

## When to Go

**Season:** March 30 through October 24 covers the full window. Early and late season (spring, early fall) avoid the worst of the heat.

**Why not summer midday?** This is an exposed quarry with limited shade. Plan an early start if you're visiting in June through August.

**Best days:** Any day Monday–Saturday works — this isn't a site where weekday vs. weekend crowding matters much, since the 40-acre quarry rarely feels packed.

## Rules and Booking

- This is private land — the BLM's 25 lb/day personal-collecting rule doesn't apply here, and there's no permit process. You pay to dig, and what you find is yours.
- Reservations aren't required, but booking online in advance is available if you want a guaranteed slot.
- Closed Sundays and July 4th — plan around these if you're routing a trip through Delta.
- Youth 16 and under must be accompanied by a paying adult in the quarry.

**[U-Dig Fossils official site](https://u-digfossils.com/)** — check current hours, rates, and any seasonal closures before you drive out.

## FAQ

**Do I need a reservation?**
No, reservations aren't required, though booking online in advance is an option if you want a guaranteed time slot, especially on a busy weekend.

**Is this good for kids?**
Yes — genuinely one of the most kid-friendly sites in this guide. Staff are used to working with children and the activity itself (splitting shale, checking each layer) is safe and engaging for most ages. Kids 16 and under need a paying adult with them in the quarry.

**Do I keep everything I find?**
Yes. Unlike BLM sites, there's no daily weight limit — everything you dig up during your paid time is yours.

**Is there cell service?**
Coverage is unreliable to nonexistent for much of the drive and at the quarry itself. The route is well-signed, so this matters less here than at a BLM site, but don't count on your phone for navigation as a backup.

**What if it's raining or the road is muddy?**
Call ahead — (435) 864-3638 — to confirm conditions before making the drive, especially outside peak season.

**Can I combine this with Topaz Mountain or Dugway in one day?**
Not comfortably. It's a legitimate additional drive from either site, not a quick detour. Better to treat U-Dig as its own trip, possibly paired with Sunstone Knoll, which is closer by.
